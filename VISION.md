# Why this site exists

The photographer's own statement of the site's purpose, inspiration and
impetus, given on 2026-10-02 during spec 019, so that every decision about
the site's design, features and flow is made with it in view. His words
follow, corrected for typos only. `CLAUDE.md`'s "Why this site exists"
distils them into the test each decision answers; this file is the
source, and where the two seem to disagree, this file wins and the
constitution is amended.

## In his words

The purpose of the site is to provide a sort of virtual installation for
my images. The reason I got into landscape photography in the first place
was that I noticed quickly that when going to an amazing place or an
incredible view, the snapshot I or someone else would take never came
close to capturing what I experienced in person. The image would be
woefully inadequate in terms of communicating the emotional experience I
had there. So my first question was, "how do I translate my experience of
being in this incredible place to a viewer of the images I take there?"

Of course I soon realized that this was impossible, but not all was lost.
Through the tools available to the photographer, visual art principles
and a sprinkling of taste and the development of my skills, I feel that I
am able to inject my emotional experience into the image by creating
something that, while representative of the reality I experienced, was
also very much a carefully and tastefully curated exaggeration of the
reality.

This doesn't mean that the viewer will experience what I experienced, far
from that, and I don't believe that is possible through the medium.
However, I am able to transmit 'something' through my images. My self
expression comes through in another way.

The site is an extension of this idea. Instead of just providing another
static gallery of images that the viewer looks at for a moment and then
moves on, the goal is to invite the viewer into my world, to give them
insight into how I think and view the world. If someone cares enough about
my images that they see on social media to then visit my site, I want to
reward them with a much deeper experience than is possible in any other
way.

My analogy is an artist's exhibit in a museum. The museum experience moves
a viewer through the artist's world. It provides context and exploration.
They are involved in the thought process of the artist, they turn a corner
and there's something else that intrigues them and they have to
physically move themself towards it as an expression of their own
curiosity.

Please note that this is a loose analogy. I don't want to create a literal
virtual museum. I want to play to the strengths of the virtual medium,
where websites allow the arbitrary hyperlinking of elements. I want the
viewer to be so immersed in a piece of content that they naturally are
inclined to move to the next one, deepening their experience, their
understanding of my world, my art, my perspective, each new piece building
on the last in some way.

So that's the main purpose of the site. My hope and intent is not that
it's just a glorified blog site but that it's something deeper, more
nuanced and intriguing. Pieces should lead into the next, always inviting
the viewer to go deeper.

I of course realize that realizing this vision will involve a lot of work
on my end, to create the content. But I just want to provide this context
to help drive and guide the design and decision making process in
developing the site, its features and flow.

I'm not trying to build a blog site from first principles, I'm trying to
build something deeper, that I also hope can be generalized such that
others can use its structure to create their own personal virtual
installation.

## What it asks of the site

Read from the statement above; each line names the part of it that it
rests on.

- **An installation, not a gallery or a blog.** A photograph arrives with
  its context — the writing, the making, the place, the other work around
  it — because the photograph alone was never enough to carry the
  experience ("another static gallery of images that the viewer looks at
  for a moment and then moves on").
- **The making is part of the work.** The photograph is "a carefully and
  tastefully curated exaggeration of the reality"; showing how it was made
  — the raw frame, the stages, the choices — is how the site lets a viewer
  into "how I think and view the world", not an appendix for
  photographers.
- **Every piece leads onward.** A page ends in an invitation that builds
  on what was just read — the next piece chosen for why it follows, not a
  generated list or a dead end ("Pieces should lead into the next, always
  inviting the viewer to go deeper").
- **Curiosity moves the visitor.** Discovery over exhaustive menus: the
  turned corner, the thing glimpsed that the reader chooses to go toward
  ("an expression of their own curiosity"). Indexes and filters exist but
  stay secondary.
- **The web's strengths, not a museum's imitation.** No rooms, walls or
  floor plans as metaphor; the medium's own power is the arbitrary link
  between any two things ("I don't want to create a literal virtual
  museum").
- **Immersion first.** Nothing on the page competes with the piece being
  read; chrome stays quiet, and anything that breaks the spell needs a
  reason.
- **Depth is the reward.** The visitor most worth designing for is the
  one who came from a photograph seen elsewhere and wants more ("reward
  them with a much deeper experience than is possible in any other way").
- **Generalisable structure.** The site's structure — the forms of piece,
  the blocks, the authoring flow — should be one that another
  photographer could use for their own installation. An aspiration, not a
  v1 requirement: it argues for keeping his content separate from the
  code and for not hard-coding him where a word in a file would do, not
  for building a product.
