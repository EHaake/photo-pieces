:::tall{src="./port-b.jpg" alt="A vendor's stall stacked to the awning, shot from low"}
Waist height, looking up. The stalls become architecture.
:::

The trick at a market is to stop photographing faces and start
photographing transactions. Hands know they're not being watched.

:::aside{src="./square.jpg" alt="A pair of hands weighing stone fruit" side="right"}
The hands were the whole story that morning. Everyone at the fruit
tables did this same test — the small lift, the half-ounce judgment —
and nobody did it the same way. I stayed at one stall through four
customers and the gesture never repeated.
:::

The vendors work in a rhythm you can set a metronome to: greet, weigh,
bag, gesture at what's best today. By the third pass down the aisle
they stop noticing the camera entirely, which is when the pictures
start.

:::row{src="./port-45.jpg" alt="A portrait of the flower vendor between customers" side="left"}
She runs the flower stall alone and has for nineteen years. Between
customers there is a stillness the portrait needed the full column of
text to sit against — the row keeps the frame and the words in
parallel, neither wrapping the other. She told me the market's real
economy runs on regulars who never haggle and tourists who always do.
:::
